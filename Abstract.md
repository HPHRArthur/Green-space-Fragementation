Urban green spaces play a critical role in maintaining ecological balance, regulating urban microclimates, and enhancing the quality of life. However, rapid urbanization and changes in land use can lead to the expansion, reduction, and fragmentation of urban green spaces, resulting in substantial changes in their spatial distribution. Predicting these changes is essential for supporting sustainable urban planning and green space management. This study proposes an integrated framework combining linear programming (LP) and the Monte Carlo method to investigate potential future changes in urban green space distribution and fragmentation. First, historical or observed green space data are used to estimate the average rates of green space expansion and fragmentation, providing probabilistic parameters for subsequent simulations. Second, linear programming is employed to incorporate planning constraints, such as financial costs and resource limitations, into the optimization process. Finally, a pixel-based Monte Carlo simulation is conducted using the current urban green space distribution map as the initial spatial condition. Each pixel represents a potential unit of spatial change, with transitions between green and non-green states representing green space expansion and fragmentation, respectively. Repeated simulations generate alternative future spatial configurations, enabling the visualization and assessment of potential changes in urban green space distribution. By integrating probabilistic simulation with constraint-based optimization, the proposed framework provides a flexible approach to exploring the uncertainty of urban green space dynamics and supporting spatially explicit urban green space planning.
